How to use this guide library

Start with the decision you need to make, not the calculator with the largest result. Open the related guide, collect the documents it names, and record which inputs are confirmed, estimated, or still unknown. Then run a base case and one less favorable case without changing several assumptions at once. This process makes the difference between the two results explainable and gives you a record to revisit when a statement, quote, rate, or household condition changes.

Evidence expected before a decision

  • Debt: use the latest statement, balance category, APR, fees, payment dates, and promotional end date.
  • Housing: use written rent terms or a Loan Estimate plus property-specific taxes, insurance, association costs, repairs, cash to close, and the expected holding period.
  • Retirement: separate dated balances and plan rules from return, inflation, spending, and timing assumptions.
  • Emergency savings: separate essential expenses, deductibles, predictable sinking funds, income-replacement time, and account-access limits.

Editorial review and corrections

We reviewed each released workflow against the public documents a reader can verify without creating a VestCalc account or sending financial entries to us. Every guide identifies its primary public sources, names material limitations, and links to a calculator that can be reproduced with local inputs. We check that a less favorable scenario remains understandable and that the article does not present a planning estimate as an approval, forecast, or personalized recommendation.

Sources, product terms, laws, and household circumstances can change. The review date records when the page scope and source links were last checked; it does not guarantee that every external fact remains unchanged. Readers can report a factual or calculation issue through the contact page. We preserve the decision context, correct the affected explanation or method, and update the review date when a material change is published.

VestCalc separates calculation methodology from editorial interpretation. Draft topics remain outside the public index until they meet the same evidence, scope, and correction standards.
